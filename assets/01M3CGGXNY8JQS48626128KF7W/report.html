1. Executive summary

324 of the 13,368 monitored domains carrying gambling content meet the Chile relevance test — 2.4% of the gambling population, and 1.1% of the 28,258 records in the export. The remaining 13,044 gambling domains carry content aimed at other markets, principally South-East and South Asia.

Findings

  • The largest replication estates in the export are irrelevant to Chile. The single biggest cluster, rooted on b777.pro, accounts for 4,226 gambling domains and yields 2 Chile-relevant records. The second, rooted on g41, yields 7 from 1,402. Ranking remediation effort by raw detection volume would direct almost all of it at operators serving other markets.
  • Chile-relevant activity is dominated by discovery, not replication. Of the reported set, 231 records arrived through discovery methods (manual review, search, video) against 93 through traced variant or redirect lineage. The inverse holds across the export as a whole, where variants and redirects are 66% of all records.
  • 20 Chile-relevant detections sit on legitimate third-party domains. A water utility, a primary school, a clinical laboratory, a medical practice, a cake shop, a timber merchant and a Spanish vocational college are all serving gambling content. These indicate compromise or paid placement, not operator-owned property, and need a notification and takedown route rather than a block order.
  • One of the three predicted successor domains has already resolved. bet364.com is present in the export as a variant of bet365.com, first recorded 2026-08-12. betsson1003 and betsala12 are still absent.
  • Detection tempo is uneven and end-loaded. 140 of the 324 reported records (43%) were recorded in the final week of the window, against a median of 18 per week across the rest.

Most consequential implication

The volume in this export is not a measure of Chilean exposure. 97.6% of monitored gambling domains are directed at other markets, and the two largest estates contribute 9 relevant records between them from 5,628. Any prioritisation built on raw counts, cluster size, or "most replicated brand" will point almost entirely away from Chile. The reported set of 324 is small enough to be worked by hand, and that is the correct unit of action.

2.2 How market relevance was determined

A record enters the reported set only if it carries gambling content and satisfies at least one relevance test. Three tests were applied in order.

  • Test A — direct market reference. The string chile anywhere in the domain including mid-string, a .cl ccTLD, a cl market marker as a subdomain or affix, or captured content citing Chile, Santiago or CLP. 128 records qualified on Test A.
  • Test B — Spanish content, uncontested. The captured reasoning quotes Spanish site text and no competing non-Spanish market indicator is present. 196 records qualified on Test B alone.
  • Test C — exclusion by competing locale. Applied before Test B. Non-Latin scripts by Unicode range, locale-specific vocabulary, and currency or country markers. 6,688 records were excluded here.

Spanish against Portuguese

The two languages were separated on vocabulary that differs between them — apuestas against apostas, jugar against jogar, dinero against dinheiro, ruleta against roleta, tragamonedas against caça-níqueis. depósito was not treated as a Spanish signal, since it is spelled identically in Portuguese and would produce false positives at scale. The same caution was applied to every other cognate surviving unchanged across the two languages.

Terminology note

The source export labels its Status field with a value carrying a legality label. That value has been relabelled to “Gambling” throughout this report. No record has been reclassified — the 13,368 records counted as gambling here are exactly the 13,368 records carrying that value in the source file, and the annex reconciles against the raw export row for row. The only content distinction this report draws is gambling against non-gambling. This report makes no assessment of whether any site is licensed, unlicensed, lawful or unlawful, and no such determination should be inferred from inclusion in any table. Where a “blocked-site list” is referred to, that describes a list supplied by the client, not a determination made here.

2.3 Replication chains

The Source field encodes lineage as Variant (parent) or Redirect (parent). Each domain was resolved recursively to its root with a cycle guard, and attributed to the root brand rather than its immediate parent, so a seventh-generation replica is counted against the brand it originated from. 3. VAT status — required input not supplied

This section could not be produced

The VAT reference file was not supplied with this job. The request carried three attachments — the report specification, the dataset manifest and the detection export. It did not carry the VAT_PAID list of registered platform URLs or the VAT_NOT_PAID_LISTED list of brands recorded as unregistered.

Without those two lists there is no basis on which to assign any domain to a VAT category. The mapping has therefore not been performed, and no VAT status, split, or per-brand VAT chart appears in this report. Assigning statuses by inference would have produced figures with no source behind them.

The rest of the analysis — market relevance, replication lineage, market attribution, infrastructure and tempo — does not depend on the VAT lists and is complete.

The three categories the mapping would populate, once the reference file is supplied, are set out below. They are shown so the gap is legible, not because any record has been assigned to them.

VAT_PAID

The domain, or any ancestor, matches a platform on the registered list. Descendants inherit.

VAT_NOT_PAID_LISTED

The domain, or any ancestor, matches a brand recorded as unregistered. Descendants inherit.

VAT_NOT_PAID

Gambling content matching neither list.

Two caveats will apply to the mapping when it is run, and both belong in front of any reader of its output rather than in a footnote.

Inheritance is an assumption, not a finding

VAT_PAID means a domain descends from a registered platform. It does not mean the domain itself sits inside that registration. A typosquat trading on a registered brand while operating outside its declared estate is indistinguishable, in this data, from a legitimate property of that brand. This is the group most needing verification, not the group needing least. The Doradobet cluster in section 4 shows the shape of the problem directly: eight character-substitution variants that would inherit their parent's status automatically.

VAT_NOT_PAID means unlisted, not confirmed unregistered

An operator may hold a registration absent from the supplied lists. The category records the absence of a match against a reference file, not a finding about the operator.

A further gap will apply: where the reference file carries a corporate entry with no consumer-brand annotation, the consumer brands operated behind it cannot be derived and will default to VAT_NOT_PAID. Conversely, corporate entries annotated with their consumer brand must have that brand token mapped as well, or every domain of that brand falls through the same way.

Two things follow from the pattern. First, the substitutions are mechanical: a→q, t→y, e→r, and two transpositions. A generator producing the next tranche is trivial to write, and defensive registration of the remaining adjacent substitutions is cheaper than detecting each one after it resolves. Second, the market attribution splits inside a single cluster: part of the estate carries Chile markers and part carries Peruvian content, which means the cluster cannot be treated as a single jurisdictional unit.

5.6 Replication depth

6.2 The residual

This analysis is scoped to records the export classifies as carrying gambling content. 9,791 records (34.6% of the export) are classified “Review needed” and a further 495 are unreachable. These are unassessed, not cleared. Of them, 153 review-needed and 13 unreachable records carry a Chile marker in the domain — they would enter the reported set on the domain test alone if their content were resolved, which would increase it by up to 51%.

The unreachable group is not inert. It includes domains that returned 502 errors, parked pages and access-denied responses at capture time; a domain unreachable on one capture is routinely reachable on the next.

6.5 Observations on the reference lists

  • The VAT reference file is absent. Section 3 sets out the consequence. This is the single largest gap in the deliverable.
  • The blocked-site list was not supplied separately. The 76-domain, 23-brand reference list embedded in the specification was used to scope brand attribution. Of the 28 P1 domains in that list, 23 appear in this export, 20 carry gambling content and 18 pass the relevance test. The 5 absent from the export entirely are: latam.betano.com, marathonbet.cl, micasinochileoficial.com, rivalo-chile.com, clbetfair.com. Their absence is a gap in monitoring coverage, not evidence that they are inactive.
  • Mixed granularity. The reference list mixes registrable domains with subdomains (be.betcris.com, blog.betway.com, chile.1xbet.com). Whether these need separate listing depends on whether blocking operates at registrable-domain or FQDN level. That should be confirmed before submission; listing them unnecessarily weakens the submission.
  • Non-gambling domains on a gambling list. aguasanjoaquin.cl appears under Latamwin in the source table but is a water utility. It is held out of the block list here for the reasons in section 6.3.
