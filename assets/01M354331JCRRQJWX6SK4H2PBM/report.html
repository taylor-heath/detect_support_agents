The export carries 1,193 rows covering 18 seed investigations and 1,193 distinct discovered domains. Classification places 1 domain in Band A (confirmed variant), 202 in Band B (probable), 95 in Band C (possible), 36 as infrastructure and 859 as weak or unrelated, for a signal yield of 17.0%.

The signal concentrates in four seeds. Betsson, 1xBet, state77, Betway account for 1,056 of the 1,193 discovered domains (88.5%) and for 168 of the 203 Band A/B variants. Where the signal sits inside those sets differs sharply by brand: Betsson leads with COMBOSQUAT; 1xBet leads with HYPHENATION; state77 leads with TYPOSQUAT; Betway leads with TLD_ROTATION.

Structural finding — read before the numbers

Because no discovered domain appears under more than one investigation and no domain was surfaced by more than one pivot, the corroboration bonus of +0.10 in §3.2 never fires anywhere in this export. Band A therefore requires a raw lexical score above 0.818, which only a Page title pivot at lexScore ≥ 0.85 or a Page HTML pivot at lexScore ≥ 0.93 can reach. A Certificate name pivot cannot produce a Band A result at all under this scoring, whatever the domain. The single-entry blocklist below is a property of the scoring model meeting a disjoint export, not evidence that only one mirror exists.

Reliability caveat on the derived seed state77.com

The extension rule derives the token state77 from the seed state77.com. Under §3.1 technique 7, Damerau-Levenshtein distance from state to state77 is exactly 2, so every domain whose SLD is state fires TYPOSQUAT. That places 35 United States government and state domains (state.gov, state.al.us through state.wy.us) into Band B. They are of 37 TYPOSQUAT-only matches in the whole export. These are false positives of the specification's own rule, not of the data; they are retained and labelled rather than silently dropped, and they must not be actioned. Excluding them, the Band B count falls from 202 to 167 and the signal yield from 17.0% to 14.1%.

Section F — Key insights

1. Yield of the sweep

1,193 domains came back from 18 seed pivots. 203 are credible variants in Bands A and B, 95 more sit in Band C awaiting re-scan, and 36 were filtered as shared plumbing under §1.4. The remaining 859 carry neither a brand token nor a gambling-lexicon term against the seed they came from. Adjusting for the 35 state.* false positives identified in A.1, the true credible count is 168, or 14.1% of the export.

Take: The export is a haystack with a measurable needle rate of roughly one credible variant in seven rows.

2. Pivot economics

Certificate name is the highest-yield pivot at 44.3% (155 variants from 350 rows) and Page title is the lowest of the productive pivots at 0.6% (1 variant from 160 rows). Favicon (Shodan default) returned 16 rows and zero variants, with 43.8% of those rows classified as infrastructure — exactly what §1.2 predicts for a pivot that identifies the host rather than the operator. The specification's weights are a prior; this export contradicts them for the two pivots that matter.

Take: Concentrate the next sweep on certificate-name queries and stop paying for favicon and title pivots.

3. Dominant mutation

COMBOSQUAT is the most frequent technique, firing on 116 domains, and Betsson uses it most (53 of its domains). Across the four large brands the templates are distinct and consistent: Betsson favours COMBOSQUAT; 1xBet favours HYPHENATION; state77 favours TYPOSQUAT; Betway favours TLD_ROTATION. A template applied this consistently is a generator, and a generator can be enumerated.

Take: The operators follow a template, and a template is predictable.

4. TLD flight

The seeds sit almost entirely on .com (1,186 of 1,193 rows). Their Band A/B/C variants land across 118 distinct suffixes, led by .com (109), .top (9), .sbs (5), .info (5). .top is the only abuse-heavy suffix under the §4.5 test (69.2% variant share across 13 domains, against a threshold of 60% and n ≥ 10). No other suffix comes close: the next-highest variant share among suffixes carrying ten or more domains is .co.uk at 36.4% across 11 domains. Note that volume is not risk — .sbs is the second-largest suffix in the export with 51 domains, but only 9.8% of them classify as variants.

Take: Blocking .com alone leaves the replica space untouched.

5. Enumeration and prediction

One brand, Betsson, runs numbered series, and it runs two of them independently: a two-digit block (betsson42, betsson69) and a four-digit block (betsson1001, betsson1002). §4.6 predicts 6 next indices across the two series. Two further numbered domains, epicbet1.com and respin1.com, are singletons and yield no series. The full candidate feed in §7.2 carries 250 rows of which 6 derive from enumeration; the rest come from TLD completion, affix permutation and typo neighbourhoods.

Take: The next mirror can be blocked before it launches — but only for Betsson, and only after a resolution check.

6. Coverage gap

§4.7 clustering produced 1002 clusters, of which 930 are singletons — short SLDs give near-orthogonal 3-gram vectors, so the specified cosine threshold of 0.35 separates most domains. One unattributed cluster of four or more members was found: the coolbet.* group (coolbet.ca, .co, .com, .mx, .pe). The §1.3 list names Coolbet only through coolbetchile.*, so the operator's primary estate is invisible to the matcher. A further 26 brand-token domains fall outside the §3.1 technique table entirely (Section E.4). Of the 14 seeds that needed the §1.3 extension rule, 11 produced genuinely new brand families and 3 were absorbed into families the list already names. No new cover domains were identified beyond the aguasanjoaquin.cl entry already listed.

Take: The reference list needs 12 additions before the next sweep: 11 derived brand families, the coolbet.* family, and an explicit brand-estate rule in §3.1.

Three generation decisions, stated because they shape the feed

Priority is taken literally. Only Betsson holds a Band A entry, so the specified formula scores every other brand's candidates at zero. The typo-neighbourhood generator excludes state. Loose ends

Items the specification calls for that this export cannot support, and observations that belong nowhere else.

  • §4.10 growth curve and §6.11 variant growth chart. Not computed — the export contains a timestamp column but every value is empty. No enforcement date was supplied in the configuration either, so the dashed enforcement rule the specification asks for has no value to sit at.
  • Geography. Not computed — the country_code column is empty in every row.
  • Favicon hashes. §7.3 allows an http.favicon.hash query where a non-default favicon hash was observed. The export records the Favicon (Shodan default) pivot as a found_by value but carries no hash column, so no such query can be emitted.
  • The kind column. Populated in all 1,193 rows with the single value facet_domain. It is retained for provenance but discriminates nothing.
  • The port, url and title columns. Present in the header, empty in every row. No page title was available to corroborate any Page title pivot result, which is part of why that pivot's entries could not be distinguished from noise.
  • Lexicon coverage. Several Page-title results under Betsson are Spanish and Portuguese gambling terms that the §3.1 technique-9 lexicon does not list: tragamoned.com, tragamonedar.com (truncations of tragamonedas), toproleta.com (Portuguese roleta against the listed Spanish ruleta) and sortemaxspot.com (sorte against the listed sorteo). Whether it belongs in a gambling-brand investigation at all is a question for the analyst who added it.
