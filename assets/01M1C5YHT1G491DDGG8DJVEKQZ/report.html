Scale of the analysis

648 distinct URLs were analysed over an evaluation period of 1 day (2026-08-27), of which 118 are gambling sites (18.2%), 336 are not gambling and 168 were unreachable at the time of analysis. A further 26 rows returned no classification and are carried as unknown.

The sample is large enough to reason about patterns, not isolated cases.

What is being offered

The three largest product subcategories are Casino (41, 34.7%), Slots (33, 28.0%), Football Betting (32, 27.1%).

5.9% of gambling sites (7) are confirmed as gambling without an evidenced product type, and 61.9% (73) took their subcategory from a reading of the classifier's reasoning rather than a lexicon keyword match. 261 of the 291 lexicon entries never matched this sample, leaving the weakest coverage in Slots (28 unmatched), Promotions (27 unmatched), Deposit & Withdrawal (23 unmatched).

The estate is spread across product types rather than concentrated: the largest, Casino, accounts for 34.7% of gambling sites.

Brand clustering

66 brand clusters were resolved across the 118 gambling URLs. 23 of them hold more than one domain and 43 are singletons; 15 clusters cover half the estate.

The largest, moenas, holds 7 domains across 1 subcategory, and the top five clusters together account for 24.6% of gambling URLs.

The estate is diffuse: per-URL blocking will have limited leverage, since it takes 15 separate clusters to cover half of it.

How the estate is grown and marketed

Of the 118 gambling URLs, 0 are crawler-identified variants, 48 sit behind redirect chains and 70 were reached directly.

The busiest redirect source, localcoffeemontclair.com, leads to 6 destinations; 14 clusters rotate their domains across more than one top-level domain.

The two most common signal tags are Abbreviations (41.5%), Marketing & Acquisition (15.3%).

Redirects and top-level-domain rotation keep a stable entry point while the sites behind it are replaced.

Enforcement position

No page in this sample carries a regulatory-blocking indicator: no court order, regulator notice or jurisdictional block page appears in the classifier's reasoning. The set appears to be pre-enforcement.

5 pages were unreadable behind an anti-bot or CAPTCHA interstitial. These are access controls set by the sites' own infrastructure, not enforcement action, and are counted separately.

This set is a candidate for a first enforcement pass, prioritised by cluster rather than by URL.
