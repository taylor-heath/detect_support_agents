Section D — Key insights

Scale of the analysis

The standard set holds 828 distinct URLs over an evaluation period of 10 days (2026-08-25 → 2026-09-03), of which 62 are illegal gambling sites and 0 are licensed.

The sample is large enough to reason about patterns, not isolated cases.
Status mix

Illegal gambling accounts for 7.5% of all URLs analysed and 100.0% of gambling sites (licensed plus illegal). 540 URLs were not gambling and 226 were unreachable.

Illegal operators are the majority of the gambling sites found.
Variants and brand abuse

2 brands were cloned via variant domains; the most-abused brand, mercyrewards.com, spawned 1 variants.

A handful of brands drive most of the proliferation, so enforcement is best prioritised by brand rather than by URL.
Redirects and traffic shaping

7 illegal URLs use redirect chains; the busiest source, cryptoalertscam.com, points to 2 destinations.

Redirects keep a stable entry point while the sites behind it are rotated.
Enforcement and regulatory relevance

No page in this sample carries a regulatory-blocking indicator, so the set appears pre-enforcement and is a candidate for a first pass. 3 pages matched a blocking phrase only because the crawl met an anti-bot challenge; those are access failures rather than enforcement, and are excluded.

This set is a candidate for a first enforcement pass.
