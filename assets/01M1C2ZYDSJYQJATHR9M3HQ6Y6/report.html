Executive summary

Across the observation window, 648 distinct URLs were analysed. 118 of them (18.2%) were identified as offering online gambling. Those 118 URLs resolve to 66 brand clusters, 23 of which hold more than one domain — so a substantial part of the estate is operated behind a much smaller number of brands.

5 · Key insights

Scale of the analysis

648 distinct URLs were analysed over an evaluation window of 1 day (2026-08-27), of which 118 are gambling sites, 336 are not gambling, 168 were unreachable and 26 returned an analyser error.

Take: The sample is large enough to reason about patterns rather than isolated cases, but it is a single-day snapshot and carries no trend.

What is being offered

The three largest product subcategories are Football Betting (45 sites, 38.1%), Slots (32 sites, 27.1%), Casino (19 sites, 16.1%). A further 14.4% of gambling sites are confirmed as gambling without an evidenced product type.

Take: Football Betting leads, but at 38.1% it is under the 40% mark: the estate is spread across product types rather than concentrated in one.

Brand clustering

66 clusters span the 118 gambling URLs; 23 hold more than one domain and 43 are singletons. 15 clusters cover half the estate. The largest, moenas, holds 7 domains across 1 subcategory.

Take: With 15 clusters needed to reach half the estate, the sample is diffuse and per-URL blocking will have limited leverage; enforcement against clusters is the more efficient unit, but no single cluster dominates.

How the estate is grown and marketed

0 crawler-identified variants, 48 sites (40.7%) sit behind redirect chains and 70 were reached directly. The busiest redirect source, localcoffeemontclair.com, feeds 6 gambling destinations, and 14 clusters rotate across more than one top-level domain. The two most common signal tags are Abbreviations (41.5%) and Marketing & Acquisition (15.3%).

Take: Redirects and top-level-domain rotation keep a stable entry point while the sites behind it are replaced. Many redirect sources are otherwise ordinary small-business and community domains, which is consistent with compromised sites being used as entry points.

Enforcement position

5 pages matched a regulatory-blocking phrase, but on inspection all 5 are anti-bot or CDN challenge pages rather than regulator notices or court orders; no page in this sample shows a confirmed regulatory block. All 5 matched rows are unreachable rows, not gambling rows.

Take: Nothing in this sample is already blocked, so the full gambling set is available for enforcement action. Prioritise by cluster rather than by URL.

7 · Methodology and limits

All online gambling is illegal under Thai law; there is no licensing route. This report therefore describes what is offered and by whom, and makes no licensing determination.

7.1 How each figure was produced

  1. Working set. 652 delivered rows, less 4 rows still awaiting review and 0 duplicate URLs, gives the standard set of 648 rows on which every figure is computed.
  2. Status. The analyser verdict was normalised to Gambling, Not gambling, Unreachable or Unknown. 26 rows returned an analyser error and are counted as Unknown, not as a negative finding. No row arrived under the upstream status that the configuration's audit counter watches for, so that counter is zero.
  3. Subcategory. 45 of 118 gambling rows (38.1%) were assigned by lexicon keyword match; the remaining 73 (61.9%) were inferred from the analyser's own description of the site. Inferred assignments carry low confidence and each records a one-sentence basis in the underlying data.
  4. Clustering. Seed linkage first, then identical stems, then near-miss merges at Jaro-Winkler ≥ 0.90 where one stem is a prefix of the other. 0 similarity merges were made.
  5. Signal corroboration. Jaccard similarity of signal-keyword sets at a 0.60 threshold, reported as leads only; it never merges clusters.

7.2 Limits on these figures

  • Subcategory figures are indicative, not firm. The inferred share is 61.9%, above the 30% threshold at which this configuration requires the disclosure. The lexicon is predominantly Thai-script while most domains and most analyser reasoning in this sample are Latin-script, so keyword coverage is thin by construction; the tabs with no keyword coverage at all here are Lottery, Playing card.
  • General-gambling share is 14.4%, below the 40% threshold; sites placed there are confirmed gambling with no product type evidenced, which is a finding rather than a failure.
  • Probable false-positive source. 48.3% of all Abbreviations-tab matches come from the single term BET (28 of 58 matches), above the 20% monitoring threshold. That tag's share should be read with caution.
  • 27 distinct lexicon terms matched at least once out of 291 configured; the unmatched terms are listed per tab in the appendix.
  • All records share a single decision date, so no time series, trend or growth rate can be derived from this sample.
  • Popularity rank is present for 74 of 118 gambling URLs; the ranking table covers only those.
  • The delivered dataset carries no legal-entity or entity-country field, so those columns are empty throughout, including in the blocklist feed.
  • The discovery route of manually added URLs identifies the human operator who added them. That identity is not reproduced in this report; those rows are shown as “Manual” only.
