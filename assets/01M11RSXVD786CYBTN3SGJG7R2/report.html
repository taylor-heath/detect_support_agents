1. Scale of the analysis

623 distinct URLs across an evaluation period of 1 day (2026-08-27 → 2026-08-27), of which 79 gambling, 252 not gambling and 117 unreachable. A further 175 rows are not yet analysed. 2 rows arriving as “Review needed” were excluded from the working set per the specification.

The sample is large enough to reason about patterns, not isolated cases.

2. What is being offered

The leading product subcategories are Slots (39, 49.4%), Football Betting (35, 44.3%), Casino (5, 6.3%). 0 gambling sites (0.0%) are confirmed as gambling without an evidenced product type. Every subcategory on this run was assigned by reasoning inference (100.0%); the keyword lexicon was not supplied with the job, so no lexicon keyword could be matched and the count of keywords that never matched cannot be reported.

Slots is the dominant product type.

3. Brand clustering

411 clusters span the 79 gambling URLs. 65 clusters hold more than one domain and 346 are singletons. 10 clusters cover half the gambling estate; the top 5 hold 34.2% and the top 10 50.6%. The largest, “ufabet”, holds 7 gambling domains across 1 subcategories.

A small number of operators account for most of the estate, so enforcement against clusters is far more efficient than against individual URLs.

Methodology and loose ends

Jurisdictional premise

Under Thai law all online gambling is illegal and there is no licensing route. This report therefore does not distinguish legal from illegal gambling: illegal, licensed and unlicensed appear nowhere as a classification axis, label, chart series, column header or metric name. The analytical weight sits on product subcategory and brand clustering instead.

78 rows arrived with a case-management state of ILLEGAL_GAMBLING and 0 rows arrived as Licensed gambling; both were normalised to Gambling on ingest. This is a data-quality signal about the upstream classifier, not a finding.

Clustering thresholds used

  • Seed linkage (§5.2 rule 1) takes precedence for Variant and Redirect rows with a parenthesised seed that looks like a domain.
  • Stem normalisation per §5.1: leading www. m. mobile. th. app. stripped, public suffix dropped, trailing affixes th thai aff affiliate vip official v\d+ \d+ stripped repeatedly, separators removed, and a result under 3 characters reverted to the pre-affix value.
  • Near-miss merge at Jaro–Winkler ≥ 0.90 and only where one stem is a prefix of the other. No merge below 0.90.
  • Signal-keyword corroboration (§5.3) not run: it requires lexicon matches. No cluster was merged on it in any case — §5.3 forbids that.

Loose ends

  • Keyword lexicon absent. The §2 workbook was not among the job attachments, so §2.1–§2.4, §4.2, §4.3, §6.6 and the two keyword heat maps could not be executed. Supplying the workbook would restore them.
  • Signal tags unavailable. All seven signal tabs are lexicon-defined. The only tags present come from §4.4 constraint 5, which is a reasoning rule.
  • Single evaluation date. Every row shares Updated at = 2026-08-27, so days = 1 and the per-day series has one point. No time trend is claimable.
  • 175 rows not yet analysed. These carry no classification (gateway status PENDING_URL_SCRAPPING / PENDING_DATA_ANALYSIS / ANALYSIS_ERROR) and normalise to Unknown. They remain in the working set and in the denominator of total, per §3.
  • 2 “Review needed” rows dropped per §3 step 1, and 0 duplicate URLs removed per §3 step 2.
  • Source is an author field here. initialAuthor yields “Manual (htaylor)” for most rows, so the parenthesised seed for a Manual row is a username rather than a domain. Seed linkage was applied only to Variant/Redirect rows whose seed contains a dot, so no cluster was formed on a username.
  • Confidence column unused in scoring. §6 defines no metric over Confidence; the values (gateway confidencePercent) were ingested but not aggregated.
  • country and language present but unspecified. The feed carries both per decision; §6 names no metric over them, so they are retained in the record but not reported.
  • Encoding. The feed was read as UTF-8 and no mojibaked Thai was detected. No Thai keyword matching was attempted, so §1’s encoding stop condition never applied.
