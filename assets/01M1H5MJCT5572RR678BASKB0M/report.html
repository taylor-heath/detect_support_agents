Scale of the analysis

648 distinct URLs were analysed over an evaluation period of 1 day (2026-08-27). Of these, 118 are gambling sites (18.2% of the sample), 336 are not gambling, and 168 could not be reached. A further 26 could not be classified.

The unreachable group is large — 25.9% of the sample — so the gambling count is a floor, not a ceiling: some unreachable hosts may be gambling sites that were simply down or defended at crawl time.

The sample is large enough to reason about patterns, not isolated cases.

What is being offered

The three most common product types are Football Betting 40 (33.9%); Casino 35 (29.7%); Slots 28 (23.7%). 7.6% of gambling sites are confirmed as gambling without an evidenced product type, and 61.9% of subcategory assignments come from reading the classifier's reasoning rather than from a lexicon keyword. 259 of the 286 distinct lexicon keywords never matched anywhere in the sample.

No single product dominates: the largest type, Football Betting, is 33.9% of gambling sites, under the 40% mark, so the estate is spread across product types rather than concentrated in one.

The lexicon is Thai-language and much of this sample is international and Latin-script, which is why coverage is thin: the weakest product tabs here are Slots, Lottery, Football Betting. Subcategory figures are therefore indicative.

Football Betting is the most common product type at 33.9% of gambling sites, but the estate is spread across product types rather than concentrated in one.

Brand clustering

The 118 gambling URLs resolve to 66 brand clusters. 23 of those hold more than one domain and 43 are singletons. 15 clusters cover half the gambling estate; the top 5 hold 24.6% and the top 10 hold 39.8%. The largest, moenas, holds 7 domains across 1 subcategory.

2 clusters span more than one product subcategory, and 0 pairs of stems were merged on name similarity — the grouping rests on crawler-asserted seed linkage and exact stem matching, not on fuzzy name matching.

With 15 clusters needed to cover half the estate, the estate is diffuse and per-URL blocking will have limited leverage; cluster-level action still beats it, but no small set of operators controls the sample.

How the estate is grown and marketed

Of the 118 gambling URLs, 0 are crawler-identified variants, 48 sit behind redirect chains and 70 were reached directly. The busiest redirect source, localcoffeemontclair.com, resolves to 6 distinct destinations. 14 clusters rotate across more than one top-level domain. The two most common signal tags are Abbreviations 49 (41.5%); Marketing & Acquisition 18 (15.3%).

The leading tag is provider and network branding rather than promotional or payment vocabulary, and the promotional tabs record no match at all in this sample, so there is no evidence here of a free-credit acquisition route.

Redirects and TLD rotation keep a stable entry point while the sites behind it are replaced, so enforcement aimed at destination URLs alone will be outrun by the entry points that feed them.

Enforcement position

No page in this sample carries a regulatory-blocking indicator: no court order, regulator notice, jurisdictional block page or Thai-language equivalent appears anywhere in the classifier's reasoning. The set appears pre-enforcement.

5 sites did return an access-denied interstitial, but these are anti-bot and CAPTCHA challenges set by the sites' own infrastructure, not blocking imposed by an authority. They are counted separately and not as enforcement.

This set is a candidate for a first enforcement pass, prioritised by cluster rather than by URL.
